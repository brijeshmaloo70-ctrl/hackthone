Track 4: Healthcare & Social Impact HEALTH-01  Proactive Agents for Chronic Care and Healthy Ageing Why this matters India’s life expectancy is about 71 years, but healthy life expectancy is only about 60.6, so people live more than a decade with chronic disease or disability. Hypertension and diabetes make up the bulk of chronic illness among the elderly. Care is reactive: patients are seen only when something goes wrong, and outcomes deteriorate in the gaps between visits (missed medicines, unnoticed warning signs, loneliness). Problem statement As AI agents become proactive, can they actively manage a patient’s chronic care lifecycle? Build a network of agents around one elderly patient. A patient agent calls and messages the patient in their own language, tracks medicines, vitals and symptoms, and remembers their history across months. A caregiver agent keeps family in the loop. A clinician agent prepares the doctor before each visit and escalates when trends turn dangerous. All of them share one evolving care plan. On the social side, the patient agent also supports routine, activity and social connection, so that healthy lifespan improves both clinically and socially. The frontier challenge True proactivity (the agent decides when to reach out, not just how to reply), monthslong memory of one person, coordination between agents serving different people around the same patient, and safe, well-calibrated escalation. What the 24-hour demo should show • A patient agent that initiates voice or WhatsApp check-ins on its own schedule and adapts to responses • A simulated 30-day patient timeline in which the agents catch a deteriorating trend and escalate • A doctor-facing summary of everything that happened between visits • A social-wellbeing behaviour, for example noticing isolation and nudging family or community Expected impact Shifts chronic care from reactive to proactive, cutting avoidable complications and hospitalisations and adding healthy, connected years to older adults’ lives.
